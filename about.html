About Personal HN Reader

A chronological list of stories that appeared on the Hacker News homepage, inspired by Wayne Larsen’s hckr news. Catch up on what you missed without losing stories as they fall off the front page.

Short on time?

Use top 10, top 20, or top 50% to find the highest-scoring stories. Like the original, rankings use UTC days, scale to the elapsed portion of each day, and include stories tied at the cutoff. Stories always remain in chronological order. Homepage shows the current front page; all shows every collected entry.

On a phone, tap the Filter button to reveal the options. Top 50% is the default.

Refreshing constantly?

The Last Visit line marks the boundary between new stories and stories available on your previous visit. Click it to dismiss it. It appears with all and top 50%; the other filters hide time and visit markers.

Enable auto-refresh to check every five minutes while the tab is visible. New stories wait behind a “new entries” button, and the tab title shows their count. Click the button when you’re ready to read them. Manual refresh shows them immediately.

Missed a few days?

Scroll to the bottom to load the preceding day automatically, or use load previous days. You can also select a date to jump directly into the archive and share that date’s URL. Use back to current news to return.

The daily archive mirrors the original’s available history beginning in June 2010. Current stories are updated on GitHub Pages every 30 minutes; GitHub may delay scheduled runs. Refresh fetches live points, comments, and homepage membership from the official Hacker News API.

Your reading preferences

The system theme follows your device’s light/dark setting. You can choose light or dark in settings, or use the header toggle. Settings also control HackerWeb discussion links, opening links in a new tab, showing dead stories, and new-comment highlights. Your choices stay in this browser.

External story titles open the original article. Comments and discussion-only titles open HackerWeb by default; disable that preference to use Hacker News.

Browser extensions and comment tracking

New-comment highlighting is built into this version. When you open a discussion, its current comment count is remembered. On a later visit or refresh, the comments column shows new / total, with the new count highlighted. Opening an article alone does not mark the discussion as read.

HackerWeb provides collapsible comment threads on the discussion page. The original project’s Chrome and Safari extensions are listed on the original extensions page; their original-domain integration is separate from this site.
